## Texte original

[0:04] What if the next breakthrough innovation never made it to market? Countries around the world are investing in groundbreaking research, but turning those discoveries into economic and societal impact still remains a challenge. Policy makers and economic development agencies are consistently developing new programs and interventions to support commercialization, but the results are often underwhelming. These programs either don't achieve their intended outcomes or do so at a cost that doesn't justify the expense. So where's the disconnect? It ultimately comes down to how we measure progress. For decades, technology readiness levels or TRLs have been used by governments, funders, and innovation programs to track technological maturity. But technology is only one piece of the commercialization puzzle. A breakthrough can be technologically advanced and

[0:54] still lack a validated market, a viable business model, a clear IP strategy, manufacturing readiness, or the right team. Without a shared way to measure these dimensions, every stakeholder develops their own yardstick. Innovators struggle to identify the right support at the right time. Programs lack consistent criteria for selecting and advancing ventures, and policy makers struggle to see where resources are needed most. We can't align an ecosystem if we can't measure progress consistently. Additionally, academic research brings a unique set of challenges. University research teams aren't typical startups. They often begin with a scientific discovery, not a defined market or validated customer need. They may have world-class technical expertise, but they're limited in commercial experience, and commercialization funding is rarely automatic. These teams

[1:45] must navigate a fragmented landscape of programs, grants, and support organizations to move forward. There are existing multi-dimensional frameworks that help assess commercial progress, but they aren't always designed for the specific realities of academic commercialization or to align readiness across dimensions and programs. Introducing the academic commercialization readiness level framework. The ACRL framework takes advantage of the familiarity of the TRLs to measure the technology progress while adding an ACRL level that captures the progress of the venture itself. The ACRL level assesses the five dimensions required for successful commercialization, business, product, manufacturing, IP, and team. Each dimension in the ACRL is assessed against a structured nine-level progression aligned with TRLs. The result, a shared view of commercial

[2:38] readiness that identifies not just how far an innovation has progressed, but what's holding it back and what needs to happen next because overall readiness is constrained by the least developed critical dimension. ACRL also maps funding and support mechanisms alongside readiness, connecting the gaps ventures need to address with the resources that can help them advance. The ACRL framework creates value across the entire innovation ecosystem. For innovators, it clarifies where a venture stands, reveals critical bottlenecks, and helps identify the support needed to move forward. For program leaders and funders, it enables more objective entry and exit criteria, helping programs select ventures at the right stage, define meaningful outcomes, and create clearer pathways between programs. For policy makers and ecosystem builders, it provides a way to map readiness against available support, identify gaps and

[3:31] overlaps, and make more strategic decisions about resource allocation. Three audiences, three applications, one shared framework. Academic commercialization is complex, but measuring progress shouldn't be. [music] The ACRL framework gives innovators, programs, funders, and policy makers a common language to understand readiness, better align supports, [music] and help move research towards commercial impact. One framework, one shared measure, a more aligned ecosystem.
